## 1 Abstract
Starting university or leaving secondary education is typically of a moment of high stress and development for young people as they are learning to navigate the world without the structure of most secondary education systems. There is a significant decrease in direction given to students and for most this is first time that they will living alone and away from family. This can lead to many students’ developing poor mental health and struggle to find effective coping mechanisms that can support them[2]. Student mental health support services have seen an increase in students requiring support and an increase of students’ report feeling depressed, anxious and experience high levels of stress[3]. Many contribute these feelings towards stressors such as financial inexperience, educational demands and poor organisational skills. Many platforms require significant input from users, are difficult to set up or subscription are needed to be paid for them to be effective. 
If someone were to already be overwhelmed, facing financial insecurity or unable to dedicate time to set up the platform, this ultimately negates the point of service. Instead, I hope to provide an app where users can stay organised and learn effective life skills while not being burdened. 
This can be achieved by introducing easily accessible progress areas, promoting good lifestyle habits, and understanding how certain actions can lead to burnout. Some healthy levels of stress can help promote better long-term memories and support the learning of students, so encouragement to try new skills and techniques can be added through memory logs and achievements[1]. We can also monitor to track these same habits and see where harmful habits are created, for example, students studying for extreme periods of time without breaks. I hope to achieve this by using GPS features to allow for ‘study spaces’ to be determined by users, allowing them to automatically track study periods but also be alerted when to take a break. Libraries and other common studying spaces can be included if users do not want to create their own ‘study spaces’.
Users will also have access to smart planners that can have automatic and manual entries added to help them with possible deadlines. These can be synced with pre-existing calendars and used in conjunction with the journal log. To support reflection and consistency, user will have the option to include a daily log in an audio or written format. Photos taken throughout the day can be included as well, with an option to see where they were taken on a map. And at the end of each week, a summary of their achievements and progress will be given, further enforcing those positive habits.
Users will also have the option to track their budgeting via smart categories and alerts once thresholds are reached, with an emphasis on visual representations to help convey growth and possible improvements.
Market research and testing will also be carried out throughout to see how users utilise the software, and what feature of the UI are easily understandable. This should be an app for beginners and should be easy to approach. 
Overall, while exist platforms for students and people in their early careers, I hope to provide an option to help beginners make decisions to support their health and learn new things. Existing platforms can be stage after this app, as this app should help users find works best for them and let them easily keep track of their progress.

## 4 Risks and Mitigations

### 4.1 Hardware Failure

There is always a risk of a hardware failure in the middle of development. At any stage, this could significantly affect progress and prevent deadlines from being reached. While the likelihood of this occurring is minimal, the impact would be significant if not catastrophic, especially further down the timeline. To prevent this, I will be keeping my code and research on GitLab and other cloud-based platforms. I will also be keeping a version stored to a physical disk drive This also does not limit development to my local machine and can be developed elsewhere. While there still a possible risk for reoccurrence or copies being lost, having multiple copies should allow for one to always be available.

### 4.2 Poor Estimation of Tasks
Due to the length of the project, there is minimal room for errors in the period spent on each task. If specific features were to take longer to the point where it overruns into other deadlines, this could create a knock-on effect that derails the whole plan. To mitigate this, splitting each task into its components and using a multi-factor estimation method, and then later using the historic data to evaluate the best course of action should reduce the likelihood of this occurring. Any features that have a dependency will also be addressed early on, allowing for concurrent development to exist if required.

### 4.3 Balance between the Report and Code
There is a significant risk that that the app development can overtake the completion of the report. This could make documentation more difficult to revisit as past actions could be forgotten. To mitigate this, I will be maintaining my diary and workbook that will have consistent dated updates. I will also be dedicating a timetabled slot every week for specifically the report, so it does not get overshadowed completely by development. I will be honest in my reflections and put into perspective the progress made.

### 4.4 End-User Safety

Users can be tracked via their GPS and account details can be shared amongst peers. This could introduce malicious actors who could greatly harm users. While the likelihood is minimal, the impact could be detrimental to users’ safety and well-being. All sensitive features such as GPS and public information will be highlighted to users and can be turned off at any time. Data will be stored securely, with personal data likely being stored locally to the user, and users will have limited access to sharing information. This will not completely negate possible risk but makes it harder for malicious actors to possibly gain access.

### 4.5 Sensitive Data Collection

Many of the user would be sharing account and sensitive personal data with the platform, and if this were to be compromised could be extremely dangerous for users. Similarly to the end-user’s safety, while this would be unlikely, the impact would be significant. To mitigate this, details of users will be encrypted and stored securely, passwords will not be stored in the clear and strict access controls will be implemented. Any templates will be stored separately from any user databases to avoid accidental cross over.

### 4.6 Scope Creep

Scope creep can lead to a difficulty in understanding when to stop adding features, which can result in missed deadlines or underdeveloped features. To prevent this, I will be making a clear plan of what I want to achieve and adhering to a strict schedule. After the first term, I will re-evaluate the progress made and use that evidence to plan the second term. Features will also be given a priority allowing for opportunities for non-essential features to be removed in favour of not compromising the overall quality.
